# Retention and transfer framework

## Retention
Use delayed retrieval when the learner needs knowledge after the immediate session.

1. Identify what must be retained and for how long.
2. Select retrieval prompts that require recall or reconstruction.
3. Schedule a first review after an appropriate delay.
4. Adjust later intervals using accuracy, effort, importance, and time horizon.
5. Correct errors and revisit weak items.
6. Retest after a meaningful delay.

Do not prescribe a universal fixed schedule. A short-term exam plan and a year-long operational competency require different schedules.

## Transfer
Transfer is the ability to apply learning beyond the exact practice context.

Design transfer by varying:
- surface features while preserving the principle;
- data, tools, or environment;
- constraints and edge cases;
- the order or combination of subskills;
- the decision required.

Ask the learner to select and justify an approach, not merely reproduce a memorized sequence.

## Transfer assessment
A valid transfer task should:
- be unfamiliar in at least one meaningful dimension;
- preserve the target underlying capability;
- define allowed resources;
- use explicit success criteria;
- avoid adding unrelated difficulty.

Report near transfer and far transfer distinctly where useful. Do not claim broad transfer from one changed example.
